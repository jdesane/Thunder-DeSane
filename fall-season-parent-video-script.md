# SFL Thunder 9U — Fall Season Kickoff
## Talking Head Video Script for Parents

**Estimated runtime:** 10–13 minutes
**Delivery notes:** Conversational, direct, warm but firm. Pause between sections. Talk to the camera like it's one parent across the table.

We've got a lot to be excited about, a few changes to walk through, and some expectations I want to lay out clearly — for the players, for us as coaches, and for the parents. So grab a coffee, give me about ten minutes, and let's get into it.

## 3. OUTSTANDING BALANCES & INVOICES (60–75 sec)

Now — a really important note on money, because I know that's always top of mind.

Because of the amount we raised through fundraising, **the season is covered for anyone who did not order additional items and did not have a prior balance rolling over into this season.** That's a big deal, and I want to say thank you to every family that helped make that happen.

**For anyone who does have an outstanding balance** — whether that's from add-ons or a prior rollover — here's how it's going to work:

- You will receive an **invoice on the 1st of each month** with the amount you owe.
- If you need to break that balance up into smaller payments over the course of the season, **just let me know** — we'll work with you. Don't stress about it, just communicate with me and we'll figure out a plan.

Bottom line: no one gets left behind over money. Just keep the lines open.

## 4. BUDGET — QUICK OVERVIEW & DISCLOSURES (75–90 sec)

I want to give you a quick, honest look at where the money is going this season, because you deserve transparency.

Two things to disclose up front:

**One — Thunder organization fees went up.** Last season the fee was $150 per player. This season it's **$300**. That's an organization-level increase, not something we set at the team level, but it hits our budget directly.

**Two — we're now paying for two umpires per game** instead of one. That expense has effectively doubled. It's the right call for the quality of the game at this age, but I want you to know that's a real line item that grew.

Everything else in the budget — tournaments, field time, equipment, uniforms — is being managed as tightly as we can. If any parent ever wants to sit down and look at the numbers, my door is open.

## 5. COACHING STAFF — WELCOMING DAN (45–60 sec)

Quick announcement I'm excited about — **we're adding Dan to our coaching staff this season.**

Dan brings a skill set and a level of value that's going to benefit every single kid on this team, and honestly, all of us as coaches too. We're excited to have him. You'll see him at practice and at games — give him a warm welcome when you meet him.

---

## 6. THE BIG TRANSITION — KID PITCH (60–75 sec)

Alright — let's talk baseball.

**This is a major transition year. We're moving from coach pitch to kid pitch.**

I want to set the expectation clearly right now: the game is going to look different. It's going to be **slower. It's going to be messier — especially early on.** There will be walks. There will be innings that get away from us. That is completely normal, and it's part of the developmental process at 9U.

**What I'm asking from all of you is patience** — patience with the kids as they figure this out, patience with us as coaches as we work through it, and patience with the game itself.

The kids who come out of this year having gone through the growing pains are the ones who will be way ahead next year.

---

## 7. PITCHING RULES & PLAYING TIME (90 sec)

A few things on pitching and playing time you need to know up front.

**Players will sit more this season than they did before.** That's just the reality of moving to kid pitch with strict pitch counts. We're going to track everything at a high level and stick to it.

Here's our rule of thumb for pitching: **a player needs to throw at least 50% strikes in practice to earn the opportunity to pitch in a game.** If they're not throwing 50% in practice, we'd be setting them up to fail by putting them out there with the added pressure of a live game. That's not fair to the kid.

Pitching opportunities are **earned** through:
- Practice
- Mechanics
- Control
- Confidence
- Ability to throw strikes
- Ability to handle the position mentally

We also have a **flow for how kids move through the game after they pitch** — where they play the rest of the innings, how we protect their arm, all of that. We're being intentional about it.

And this is important: **pitching development takes time.** There will be walks. There will be rough innings. Coaches need room to let players work through some adversity — that's how they learn.

---

## 8. PLAYER EFFORT & ATTITUDE (75 sec)

Let's talk about what we expect from the players themselves.

**We do not expect perfection. We do expect effort.**

- Hustle on and off the field.
- Run everything out.
- Be locked in when it's your turn.

Errors and strikeouts are baseball — they will happen and that's fine. **Lack of effort, bad attitude, disrespect, or not paying attention repeatedly — that's not.**

On body language and attitude:
- **No throwing helmets or equipment.**
- **No crying or shutting down** because of an error or a strikeout.
- **No blaming umpires, teammates, coaches, or the field.**

We're teaching them how to handle adversity — that starts with how they carry themselves.

---

## 9. POSITIONS (30 sec)

Quick note on positions: **positions are not owned by any player.** We're going to move kids around this fall so we can figure out where each one is developing best. That's what a development year looks like.

---

## 10. PRACTICE & AT-HOME WORK (75 sec)

**Practice expectations:**
- Be on time and ready to go.
- If your player is going to miss, please let a coach know as early as possible.

**At-home development:**

Team practice alone will not develop every skill. That's just the truth of it. Kids who put in a little extra work at home will naturally develop faster.

We're not asking for hours. Encourage:
- Playing catch
- Tee work
- Dry swings
- Ground balls
- Pitching mechanics

**Fifteen to twenty productive minutes a few times a week makes a huge difference.**

---

## 11. PARENTS IN THE STANDS (90 sec)

This is a big one, so I'm going to be direct.

**Sideline expectations for parents and grandparents:**

- **Cheer for our team. Not against the other team.**
- Positive encouragement only.
- **No yelling instructions to players** during at-bats or while they're on defense. **One voice** should be coaching that player, and that's the coach's voice.
- Let the coaches coach.

**On umpires:**
- No arguing with umpires from the stands.
- No yelling about balls and strikes.
- No sarcastic comments.
- If there's a legitimate issue, we'll handle it.

Our kids need to see adults model the same sportsmanship we're asking of them. Full stop.

**On other teams and other players:**
- No making fun of other players.
- No comments about another child's ability.
- No criticizing another team's coaches or parents where kids can hear it.

We want Thunder families representing this organization the right way — every game.

---

## 12. TALKING WITH THE COACHES (90 sec)

Communication — I want this to stay open and productive all season.

**We are always happy to talk with you about your own child:**
- Development
- What your player needs to improve
- How you can help at home
- What we see as his strengths

**What we're not going to do is have emotional, in-the-moment playing-time discussions.** If something bothers you, give it a little time, then come have a productive conversation with us. That's how we keep things respectful and actually get somewhere.

**And on other players** — parents can absolutely ask about their own child. But we're not going to discuss another player's:
- Playing time
- Ability
- Position
- Performance
- Family

Please keep those conversations focused on your player.

---

## 13. ATTENDANCE & BEING PART OF A TEAM (75 sec)

**Attendance:**

Please **update availability as early as possible.** Rosters, pitching plans, lineups, defensive rotations — all of it depends on us knowing who's actually going to be there. Last-minute cancellations impact the whole team.

Baseball is a team commitment — please treat scheduled games and tournaments as commitments whenever it's reasonably possible.

**Being part of a team:**

There will be weekends where your player has a huge game. There will be weekends where he struggles. He'll play his favorite position sometimes. Sometimes he won't. He'll sit an inning. He'll get the big opportunity.

**We need families supporting the entire team** — not only zeroed in on their own kid's stat line.

---

## 14. COACHING DECISIONS (45 sec)

Last piece before I wrap up.

**We will make decisions based on what we believe is best for the overall team.** You will not always agree with every decision we make — and that's okay. What matters is that we maintain trust and communicate respectfully with each other.

---

## 15. CLOSE — What Success Looks Like (45–60 sec)

Here's how I'll leave it.

**Success this fall isn't only wins and losses.**

Success is watching these kids handle the transition to kid pitch. Watching them hustle. Watching them pick a teammate up after a bad inning. Watching them earn something through effort.

We're going to **coach them hard, support them harder, and give them every opportunity we can to become better baseball players and better teammates.**

Thanks for trusting us with your kid this fall. Let's go have a great season.

**Go Thunder.**
